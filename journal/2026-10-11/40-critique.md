## What each variant gets right / wrong

**a (goal-bar hero, stat tiles):** the stills match the brief claim by claim -
"15 / 4,703 / 4.3" tiles, "46 of 52", bar filled to the computed 88% with the
tick visibly inside the fill at 83%, "3 ahead of pace - 88% of goal",
"day 304 of 365", four shelf titles. Every printed number reconciles. The
portrait re-render fixed the huddle: sections spread down the page. Nits: the
delta line is small next to the bar it explains, and the bare shelf ratings
("5", "4.5") could be misread as counts until the "avg rating" tile gives
context.

**b (pace-forward):** the square still is the best single frame of the day -
the pace line as the hero, everything else quiet. But the hero design only
celebrates one reader: the template's own props make "16 behind pace" (Kami's
line, the common case mid-year) the biggest element on the card, printed DIM -
a hero of bad news. On landscape, "88% of goal" sits directly under the bar's
unfilled tail and reads as if it labels the remainder. Variant a keeps the
delta prominent but supporting, so the card works whatever the delta's sign.

## Decision: SHIP a

## If ship: what a human polish pass should look at first

The shelf ratings column ("5", "5", "4.5", "4") - a stranger can read it as
counts; "4.5/5" or a labelled column would kill the ambiguity. Second, the
portrait gaps are even but generous; a slightly larger row cap on tall
canvases would fill them with content instead of air.

## Why not b, in one line

b's hero only works when the reader is ahead; a wrap-up card has to serve the
behind-pace majority too.
