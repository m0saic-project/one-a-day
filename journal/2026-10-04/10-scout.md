## Candidates (3-5, best first)

### 1. Cubing: the "Generated By csTimer" average, as a card/clip
- Who: speedcubers. SpeedSolving.com (forum competitions, "races"), the WCA community.
- The construct: the average of 5 / 12 / 100 with the best and worst solve dropped, written in parentheses. csTimer is the shared tool; its "Generated By csTimer on <date> ... avg of 12: 28.71 ... Time List: 1. 27.39 <scramble> @<timestamp>" block is the artifact everyone pastes. The WCA scoresheet writes the same thing: `(20.31) 30.71 26.32 (32.16) 25.74`.
- The recurring need: after a session or a PB, show the average as a picture instead of a pasted wall of text with scramble strings; "race to sub-X" threads post PBs and goals for single / ao5 / ao12 / ao100 over and over. One card (or a short build-up clip, one solve per beat) per PB average.
- Evidence:
  - [oh race thread](https://www.speedsolving.com/threads/oh-race-d-yescubing-and-cubelite.95050/): members paste raw csTimer blocks ("avg of 5: 27.59", dropped solves in parentheses, scrambles and timestamps) as their progress report; the same thread carries PB/goal tables typed by hand (single 14.93, ao5 20.63, ao12 24.88, ao100 26.90).
  - [New Speedsolving Timer](https://www.speedsolving.com/threads/new-speedsolving-timer.97451/): the forum's own timer (Aug 2026) tracks PB single / Ao5 / Ao12 / Ao50 / Ao100, imports from csTimer, exports CSV/JSON, and "a PB notification pops up the moment you set a new record". The moment exists; nothing turns it into a picture. A member in the same thread reports csTimer's export giving a .txt rather than the .json the importer expects, so the csTimer text is the form people actually hold.
  - Same thread: blindfolded events show Mo3 instead of rolling averages, because one DNF wipes out an Ao5. The format knowledge is community-specific, which a generic chart tool gets wrong.
- Why m0saic fits: pure rectangles and numbers. Props mirror the artifact: `event`, `kind` (ao5 / ao12 / mo3), `solves[]` (seconds, DNF, +2), `date`, optional `previousPb`. A bar per solve scaled to the slowest, the dropped best/worst shown in parentheses with a hollow bar, the average as the headline, the delta against the old PB. No media needed, deterministic, batchable (one card per PB). The computation (drop best/worst, DNF rules, 2-decimal rounding) is the real content.
- Risks: timer apps already render share cards (seen in search results only, not opened), so the angle is "from the text everybody already pastes", not "first card". Looks similar to day 12's time-drop card (times and deltas); differentiate on the dropped-solve convention and the bar per solve. Scramble strings are noise; leave them out.

### 2. VGC team sheet from the Pokemon Showdown export
- Who: competitive Pokemon (VGC) players; Pokemon Showdown, Victory Road team reports, tournament pages.
- The construct: the Showdown export text (`Species @ Item / Ability: / EVs: / Nature / - Move`), documented as one of three team formats ([docs](https://www.mintlify.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/simulator/teams)). Open team sheets are used at VGC events.
- The recurring need: every event a player prints or posts a six-Pokemon sheet; [Victory Road](https://victoryroad.pro/sv-reports/) compiles winners' teams per regulation, and a [Chrome extension](https://extscope.org/extension/hhmgmifkiefbakippglilgcfnndlkbab) exists only to fill a PDF form from the paste (58 users).
- Why m0saic fits: six cells x (species, item, ability, four moves) from a paste; a clean 3x2 grid.
- Risks: without sprites it is a text table; Pokemon IP and a large name vocabulary; parsing a paste is more work than the visual is worth for a demo.

### 3. Disc golf round / league card from the UDisc CSV
- Who: disc golfers and league organisers; UDisc app and [UDisc forum](https://forum.udisc.com/t/export-uid-in-csv/156173).
- The construct: UDisc's CSV, `PlayerName,CourseName,LayoutName,Date,Total,+/-,Hole1..HoleN`, with a "Par" row. The forum thread shows a league organiser building a custom ranking from it and a member who normalised it into SQL.
- The recurring need: league night results and personal rounds; organisers re-process the CSV by hand. A [GPX request](https://forum.udisc.com/t/please-add-a-gpx-export-option-for-rounds/742586) confirms CSV is the only export.
- Why m0saic fits: hole-by-hole grid, birdie/par/bogey colouring, +/- headline, one card per player per night. The props are the CSV columns.
- Risks: UDisc already has share cards and CardCast (from search snippets); the hole grid echoes day 10's grid and day 8's attempt board; the CSV only carries par in the Par row.

### 4. Climbing season recap from a logbook CSV
- Who: boulderers and route climbers on Mountain Project, 8a.nu / Vertical-Life, theCrag, KAYA.
- The construct: the ticks CSV (grade, style, date, route); theCrag documents [exporting from 8a.nu](https://www.thecrag.com/en/article/export8a) and importing it.
- The recurring need: an end-of-year "sends by grade" card.
- Why m0saic fits: a grade pyramid is stacked rectangles.
- Risks: the evidence is only that export and import exist; I found no one asking for a graphic. Grade systems differ (V, Font, YDS, French): many knobs.
